The sequence question is the right one. Let me answer it directly.

---

**What creates confidence fastest without misrepresentation:**

Confidence in a hiring context has a specific structure. It isn't built by accumulating positive signals. It's built by eliminating the dominant doubt.

A recruiter arriving at an unknown portfolio carries one primary doubt:

> Is this person real, or just a good presenter?

Everything else is secondary. Skills, experience, contact info — none of that matters until that doubt is resolved. Which means Recruiter Mode's sequence problem is actually a credibility problem, not an information architecture problem.

---

**The sequence that resolves it:**

**First: Show something only someone who built it could show.**

Not a screenshot. Not a description. Something that demonstrates the gap between having an idea and having executed it. The Observatory itself is the strongest candidate — the gap between "graph-native knowledge architecture" as a concept and what's actually running in the wireframe is immediately visible to anyone who's shipped software. That gap is the credibility signal.

**Second: Name the rare thing directly.**

Not a list of skills. One sentence that says what kind of engineer this is. Your candidate — "thinks in systems rather than features" — is close. But it needs one concrete anchor or it reads like every senior engineer's LinkedIn summary. The anchor should be something unexpected. The Observatory's constitutional law hierarchy governing a portfolio is unexpected. That's the anchor.

**Third: Remove friction from the decision.**

Resume and contact last, but framed as: "You've seen enough to have a question. Here's how to ask it." Not "here's my credentials." The recruiter who reaches card three already has enough to decide. The card's job is to make yes frictionless.

---

**The sequence in Observatory terms:**


Eliminate dominant doubt    →  Profile + live evidence
Name the rare thing         →  Flagship Projects  
Make decision frictionless  →  Resume & Contact


That maps directly to your three cards. But the *content* within each card needs to serve doubt-elimination first, information-delivery second.

---

**The one thing the wireframe doesn't yet answer:**

What is the live evidence in Profile Overview that only someone who built it could show?

The Observatory architecture is the obvious candidate. But is it presented in a way that a recruiter — who may not know what a graph-native knowledge architecture is — can immediately recognize as evidence of unusual capability?

That's the content question Session J needs to resolve first. Everything else in the mode is already architecturally sound.
