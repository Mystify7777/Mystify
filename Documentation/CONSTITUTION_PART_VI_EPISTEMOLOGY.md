# THE MYSTIFY OBSERVATORY CONSTITUTION — PART VI: FACTS, VALIDITY, AND EPISTEMOLOGY

This Part governs knowledge about reality: how information participates, how validity is originated and traced, and how interpretation differs from computation.

## Article 31 — Facts
- Facts are informational roles, not ontology classes or node types.
- Information becomes a fact when it is evaluated during outcome production.
- Identity remains stable; roles are positional.
- The same information may participate as a fact in one context and not in another.

## Article 32 — Evaluated and Applied Information
- Information evaluated during interpretation functions as fact.
- Rules, conditions, and frameworks are applied rather than evaluated; the evaluated and applied roles are distinct.
- The same information may participate in either role depending on context.
- Role transitions shall not imply identity transitions.

## Article 33 — Identity and Role
- Identity is stable; role is positional.
- Roles derive from participation rather than existence.
- Information may participate in multiple roles throughout its lifecycle.
- Role changes shall not be interpreted as identity changes; recursion is permitted and does not imply contradiction.

## Article 34 — Authoritative Information
- Authoritative Information is information whose validity originates within a recognized authority.
- Production by authority alone is insufficient; the origin of validity determines authority.
- Recognition determines legitimacy; authoritative vs. produced information are distinct concepts.

## Article 35 — Validity
- Validity may originate or be inherited; these are distinct states.
- Inherited validity shall not be mistaken for originated validity.
- Validity derives from recognition rather than proximity and shall remain traceable to its origin.

## Article 36 — Recognition
- Recognition is the responsibility of Governance.
- Recognition determines which authorities may originate validity.
- Recognition does not imply consumption; authorities must acknowledge recognized validity but are not obligated to consume it.
- Recognition and dependency are distinct concepts.

## Article 37 — Interpretation
- Interpretation is performed by Rendering Authorities.
- Interpretation applies owned rules to available information and renders outcomes.
- Interpretation carries ownership and responsibility; it originates outcomes.

## Article 38 — Computation
- Computation produces outputs but does not own rules or originate outcomes.
- Computation executes supplied procedures; computation and interpretation are distinct.

## Article 39 — Relationship Between Interpretation and Computation
- Interpretation = Computation + Ownership of Rules.
- Ownership transforms computed outputs into outcomes; responsibility transforms execution into interpretation.
- Processors compute; authorities interpret.

## Article 40 — Rendering Products
- Rendering products derive identity from their production contexts and are distinct from ontological classes.
- Ontological classes survive the removal of producers; rendering products may not.
- Production context and ontological identity are distinct concepts.

## Article 41 — Recursive Participation
- Information may participate recursively across architectural chains (outcomes as facts, effects in interpretation).
- Role changes do not imply identity changes; recursive participation is constitutional.

## Article 42 — Failure and Absence
- Absence of information does not justify invention.
- Incomplete information shall not transfer authority; qualified outcomes are preferable to fabricated certainty.
- Unknown and insufficient states possess architectural legitimacy; certainty shall not be manufactured.

## Article 43 — Knowledge and Ownership
- Knowledge and ownership are distinct; meaning and governance are distinct.
- Dependency and authority are distinct; participation and ownership are distinct.
- Structural proximity shall not confer identity or authority.

## Article 44 — Authoritative Boundaries
- Origin of validity shall remain discoverable and legitimacy traceable.
- Authorities shall not claim validity they did not originate.
- Inherited validity shall remain identifiable; authority shall remain accountable to origin.
