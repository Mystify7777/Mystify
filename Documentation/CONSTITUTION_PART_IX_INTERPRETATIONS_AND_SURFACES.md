# THE MYSTIFY OBSERVATORY CONSTITUTION — PART IX: INTERPRETATIONS AND SURFACES

This Part describes how the same Observatory presents itself through different perspectives. Interpretations are epistemological: they change surfacing and voice, not truth.

## Article 77 — Interpretations
- Interpretations are perspectives upon the same Observatory.
- Interpretations are not themes, not independent environments, and they do not possess independent truths.
- Reality remains singular; presentation may vary but truth does not.

## Article 78 — Shared Reality
- All interpretations inhabit one shared reality: discoveries are discoveries, constraints remain constraints, truth remains truth.
- Modes may influence surfacing but shall not influence authority, legality, or topology.
- Interpretation affects visibility rather than existence.

## Article 79 — Recruiter Interpretation
- Recruiter Mode exists to establish trust.
- Governing principles: Clarity, Warmth, Confidence.
- Identity precedes capability; capability precedes depth; understanding precedes admiration.
- Recruiter interpretation favors understanding over mystery; complexity is subordinate to clarity.

## Article 80 — Explorer Interpretation
- Explorer Mode exists to cultivate presence.
- Governing principles: Discovery, Habitation, Awareness.
- Mystery shall serve understanding; discovery reveals existing truths.
- Progression remains subordinate to presence; the Observatory should feel inhabited rather than gamified.

## Article 81 — Engineer Interpretation
- Engineer Mode exists to reveal truth.
- Governing principles: Observability, Transparency, Explainability.
- Reality must remain distinguishable from models; metrics must be observed, modeled, simulated, or explicitly unknown.
- Fabricated telemetry is prohibited.

## Article 82 — Surface Independence
- Surfaces express interpretations but do not own truth.
- Surface changes shall not alter constitutional meaning; presentation is subordinate to reality.
- The same truth may possess multiple surfaces; surface diversity does not imply ontological diversity.

## Article 83 — Shared Implementations
- Applications share one implementation; interpretations may vary in naming, accent, terminology, and cosmetic identity.
- Interpretations shall not vary content, hierarchy, functionality, or truth.
- Implementation singularity preserves reality.

## Article 84 — Windows
- Windows are environmental expressions; applications do not own windows.
- Window behavior belongs to the Window System; ownership of application meaning does not imply ownership of environmental behavior.

## Article 85 — The Observatory Index
- The Observatory Index represents topology and is not merely a menu.
- The Index serves navigation, discovery, environmental awareness, and entity interaction.
- Topology takes precedence over navigation; the Index reveals existence rather than destinations.

## Article 86 — Signals Across Interpretations
- Signals may vary in expression across interpretations but shall not vary in truth.
- Interpretation influences voice, not validity; observability remains mode-independent.

## Article 87 — Environmental Unity
- All interpretations belong to one Observatory; no interpretation shall supersede another or possess privileged truth.
- Differences in perspective shall not imply differences in reality; plurality preserves unity.

## Article 88 — Surface and Reality
- Reality precedes surface; surfaces reveal reality but do not create it.
- Visibility and existence are distinct; presentation and truth are distinct.
- Structural resemblance shall not be mistaken for ontological ownership.
