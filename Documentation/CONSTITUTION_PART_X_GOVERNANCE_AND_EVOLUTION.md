# THE MYSTIFY OBSERVATORY CONSTITUTION — PART X: GOVERNANCE AND EVOLUTION

This Part constitutionalizes how the Observatory governs itself, how its laws evolve, and how discoveries become authoritative.

## Article 89 — Constitutional Authority
- The Constitution possesses the highest architectural authority.
- Implementations and documents derive legitimacy from the Constitution.
- No implementation shall possess constitutional authority; constitutional principles govern implementations rather than emerge from them.

## Article 90 — Evolution
- The Observatory evolves through discovery and adversarial pressure.
- Architectural evolution favors refinement over replacement; novelty alone shall not justify change.

## Article 91 — Adversarial Pressure
- Architectural claims shall be subjected to adversarial pressure to reveal distinctions rather than defend preferences.
- Survival under pressure grants confidence; failure under pressure grants understanding.
- Dissolution is preferable to false permanence.

## Article 92 — Constitutionalization
- Constitutional authority is earned, not granted by repetition or popularity.
- Distinctions must demonstrate independent necessity and survive adversarial examination before elevation.

## Article 93 — Glossary Evolution
- Vocabulary exists to express distinctions; terms shall justify their existence.
- Terms that own no independent topology shall dissolve; precision takes precedence over familiarity.

## Article 94 — Outcome Taxonomy
- Architectural investigations may produce: Refinement, Deepening, Generalization, Excavation, Displacement, Empty Dissolution, Absorptive Dissolution.
- These outcomes describe how distinctions evolve; dissolution does not equal failure.

## Article 95 — Necessity
- Architectural necessity supersedes elegance; the absence of necessity outweighs beauty.
- Existence in language does not guarantee architectural existence.

## Article 96 — Recognition
- Recognition belongs to Governance and grants legitimacy without implying dependency or mandatory consumption.
- Recognition establishes validity rather than obligation.

## Article 97 — Diagnostic Principles
- Architectural pressure shall favor reusable tests (e.g., Necessity Test, Authority Prerequisite Test, Verdictification Test, Intersection Test, Originated Validity Test).
- Tests are tools, not authorities; no diagnostic possesses constitutional supremacy.

## Article 98 — Historical Records
- Historical records possess explanatory authority but not constitutional authority.
- Chats, notebooks, and archives explain discovery; they do not govern the future.
- History shall illuminate the Constitution, not replace it.

## Article 99 — Watching
- Certain distinctions may remain unresolved and possess architectural legitimacy.
- Unresolved questions shall be watched rather than prematurely constitutionalized.
- Watching terms remain outside constitutional authority until sufficient pressure has been applied.

## Article 100 — Amendments
- Amendments refine the Constitution and shall preserve coherence.
- Replacement requires demonstrable architectural necessity; the burden of justification lies with the proposer.
- Amendments shall emerge through discovery rather than decree.

## Article 101 — Discovery
- The Observatory is discovered more often than invented; architectural truth is uncovered through pressure.
- The Constitution records discoveries; no document claims finality over reality.
- Understanding remains provisional; discovery remains perpetual.

## Article 102 — Humility
- No principle is immune to examination; constitutional authority grants responsibility, not infallibility.
- The purpose of the Constitution is understanding; understanding takes precedence over certainty and pride.

---

This Part formalizes how the Observatory learns and changes. It embeds adversarial testing, recognition, and humility into the system so that the Constitution remains a living, discoverable artifact rather than a static decree.
