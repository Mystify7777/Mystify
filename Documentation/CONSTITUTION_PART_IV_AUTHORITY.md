# THE MYSTIFY OBSERVATORY CONSTITUTION — PART IV: AUTHORITY

This section moves the Constitution from identity into authority: the first structural layer where the Observatory stops behaving as philosophy and begins to behave as a constitutional system.

## Article 8 — Authority
Authority exists to render outcomes, perform consequences, govern recognition, and maintain state.

- Authority is partitioned.
- No authority is universal.
- No authority shall possess ownership beyond its designated domain.
- Authority shall derive from ownership rather than proximity.

## Article 9 — Rendering Authorities
Rendering Authorities own rules and bear responsibility for interpretation.

- Rendering Authorities evaluate available information according to their owned rules and render outcomes.
- An outcome shall originate from exactly one Rendering Authority.
- Multiple authorities may contribute facts, but interpretation shall remain singular.

## Article 10 — Enforcement Authorities
Enforcement Authorities own consequences and are responsible for execution rather than interpretation.

- The authority to interpret and the authority to enforce are distinct.
- Consequences are enforcement acts performed by Enforcement Authorities within their designated domains.
- Co-locating interpretation and enforcement in an implementation must not collapse the constitutional distinction.

## Article 11 — State Domains
State Domains own classes of authoritative state.

- Ownership of state is distinct from the authority to mutate it.
- Location or storage does not determine state ownership or jurisdiction.
- Mutation authority and state ownership may belong to different authorities.

## Article 12 — Governance
Governance owns recognition.

- Recognition determines which authorities may originate validity.
- Recognition does not imply mandatory consumption; authorities may acknowledge recognized validity without being obligated to consume it.
- Authorities are obligated to acknowledge recognized validity where applicable.

## Article 13 — Registries
Registries are holders of meaning.

- Registries document authoritative meaning within their domains.
- Registries know; they do not render outcomes or perform consequences.

## Article 14 — Engines
Engines are implementations of authority.

- Engines decide; engines are implementations, not owners of meaning or state.
- Authority belongs to the constitutional role, not the implementation.
- Implementations may change without altering constitutional ownership.

## Article 15 — Graph Layer
The Graph Layer owns connectivity and grants reachability.

- Connectivity implies topology, not meaning, legality, or authority.
- The Graph Layer provides reachability and structure; interpretation remains the responsibility of authorities.

## Article 16 — Separation of Roles
Rendering Authority, Enforcement Authority, State Domain, Governance, Registries, and the Graph Layer represent distinct constitutional roles.

- Implementation overlap shall not collapse role boundaries.
- Role and implementation are distinct concepts.
- Structural proximity shall not confer identity or jurisdiction.

## Article 17 — Single Origin Principle
Every outcome shall originate from exactly one Rendering Authority.

- Multiple authorities may contribute information, but interpretation must remain singular.
- Authority shall be coordinated rather than negotiated.
- Ambiguity of ownership is architectural corruption and shall be treated as such.

## Article 18 — Ownership and Jurisdiction
Ownership determines jurisdiction.

- Dependencies and consumption do not confer ownership or authority.
- Proximity does not confer jurisdiction.
- Authority belongs to what is owned rather than to what is merely used.
- The ownership principle remains under continued observation and refinement.

With Part IV (Authority) established, the Constitution has set identity, fundamental principles, interpretations, and authority.
