# THE MYSTIFY OBSERVATORY CONSTITUTION — PART VII: TRAVERSAL AND TOPOLOGY

This Part treats questions, reachability, and the interrogative topology that organizes the Observatory.

## Article 45 — Topology
Topology represents the structure of reachability within the Observatory.

- Topology does not define meaning, legality, or truth.
- Topology enables questions to be asked; answers belong to authorities rather than to topology.

## Article 46 — The Graph Layer
The Graph Layer owns connectivity.

- Connectivity grants reachability but does not imply meaning, authority, or legality.
- The Graph Layer provides infrastructure; interpretation remains the responsibility of authorities.

## Article 47 — Reachability
- Reachability determines whether something may be reached.
- Reachability does not determine meaning, validity, or visibility.
- Reachability and interpretation are distinct concepts.

## Article 48 — Questions
- Questions are first-class architectural citizens.
- Questions determine the nature of traversal; objects are subordinate to the questions asked about them.
- The identity of a traversal derives from the question it serves rather than from the path it follows.

## Article 49 — Traversal Classes
Traversal Classes categorize traversals by the architectural question being asked and by the authority responsible for answering it.

- Traversal Classes are interrogative in nature; they are not paths, coordinators, or relationship types.

## Article 50 — Owned Traversal
Owned Traversal asks: "What do I own?"

- Ownership determines the answer; the Local Registry is responsible for answering.

## Article 51 — Reference Traversal
Reference Traversal asks: "What does this connect to?"

- Connectivity determines reachability; meaning belongs to the destination.
- Reachability does not imply interpretation.

## Article 52 — Justification Traversal
Justification Traversal asks: "Why is this permitted to exist?"

- The Validation Engine renders answers; traversal grants access and authority grants explanation.

## Article 53 — Derivation Traversal
Derivation Traversal asks: "How was this computed?"

- The Derivation Engine renders provenance and computation answers; derivation concerns provenance rather than ownership.

## Article 54 — Visibility Traversal
Visibility Traversal asks: "May I see this?"

- The Activation Engine is responsible for answering.
- Visibility is distinct from existence, meaning, and reachability.

## Article 55 — Path and Class
- Traversal Paths and Traversal Classes are distinct.
- Different questions may follow identical paths; identical paths do not imply identical traversals.
- Question identity supersedes path identity.

## Article 56 — Reachability and Answers
- Traversal grants reachability; authorities render answers.
- Reachability does not imply understanding; reachability and explanation are distinct.

## Article 57 — Meaning
- Meaning belongs to Registries.
- Connectivity belongs to the Graph Layer.
- Interpretation belongs to Authorities.
- Change belongs to Consequences.
- These domains are distinct; no domain shall absorb another.

## Article 58 — Topological Neutrality
- Topology is neutral: it does not possess truth, meaning, or authority.
- The graph provides possibility rather than judgment.

## Article 59 — Interrogative Architecture
- The Observatory organizes around questions rather than objects.
- Questions reveal ownership, legality, visibility, derivation, and meaning.
- Objects exist within the architecture; questions govern how the architecture is understood.

## Article 60 — Cross-Link Principle
- Traversal legality is independent of interpretation.
- Interpretations may alter surfacing but shall not alter authority, legality, or topology.
- Modes shall not alter truth.

## Article 61 — Separation of Domains
- Reachability and meaning are distinct; meaning and authority are distinct; authority and consequences are distinct.
- Visibility and existence are distinct; connectivity and truth are distinct.
- Structural proximity shall not confer identity.
