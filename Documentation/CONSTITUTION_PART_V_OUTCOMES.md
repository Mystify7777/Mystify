# THE MYSTIFY OBSERVATORY CONSTITUTION — PART V: OUTCOMES, CONSEQUENCES, EFFECTS, AND STATE

This Part defines how reality changes within the Observatory: what is rendered, what is enforced, what happens downstream, and what constitutes state.

## Article 19 — Outcomes
An outcome is the result rendered by a Rendering Authority.

- Outcomes are products of interpretation.
- Outcomes do not possess enforcement authority and do not, by themselves, perform changes.
- Identical outcomes may arise from distinct informational foundations.
- An outcome's meaning is derived from the Rendering Authority that rendered it.

## Article 20 — Outcome Basis
Every outcome possesses an Outcome Basis: the informational-sufficiency foundation on which the outcome was rendered.

- Outcome Basis measures informational sufficiency, not absolute truth.
- Outcome and Outcome Basis are distinct concepts.
- Identical outcomes may possess different Outcome Bases.
- Outcome Bases may be classified as: Complete, Partial, Insufficient — these classifications describe sufficiency, not evaluation mechanisms.

## Article 21 — Consequences
Consequences are enforcement acts performed by Enforcement Authorities.

- Consequences are authoritative and belong to the authorities that perform them.
- Consequences are distinct from outcomes and distinct from effects.
- Consequences may exist without producing immediate effects; the existence of effects does not define consequences.

## Article 22 — Effects
Effects are downstream results whose existence depends upon consequences.

- Effects possess no authority and do not originate validity.
- Effects may become inputs to subsequent interpretations but do not thereby acquire authority.
- Multiple effects may originate from a single consequence; effects may form chains.
- Authority does not propagate through chains of effects.

## Article 23 — State
State represents present reality within a State Domain.

- State ownership belongs to State Domains.
- State mutation authority and state ownership are distinct concepts.
- Location or storage shall not determine ownership or jurisdiction.
- State classes are defined by ownership rather than persistence mechanisms.

## Article 24 — State Transitions
State transitions are changes occurring within state.

- State transitions are effects rather than authorities.
- State transitions frequently arise from consequences, but state transitions do not define consequences.
- Observed patterns are informative but shall not be treated as constitutional definitions.

## Article 25 — Chains
The general chain of architectural change is:

Outcome
↓
Consequence
↓
Effect

- Effects may participate in future renderings.
- Role transitions do not imply identity transitions.
- Authority shall not emerge merely from participation in chains.

## Article 26 — Recursive Chains
Information may participate in multiple stages of architectural evolution.

- An outcome may later function as a fact.
- An effect may later participate in interpretation.
- Identity remains stable; roles are positional.
- Recursion is permitted and not inherently contradictory.

## Article 27 — Separation of Change
Constitutional separations shall be preserved:

- Interpretation and enforcement are distinct.
- Enforcement and effects are distinct.
- Effects and authority are distinct.
- State and state mutation are distinct.

Observed patterns must not collapse these separations; structural proximity shall not confer identity.

## Article 28 — Legality
Legality determines permission; it is rendered through interpretation.

- Legality does not itself perform consequences.
- Legality and enforcement are distinct concepts.
- Legality outcomes may be classified as: Legal, Illegal, Undetermined.
- Legality describes permission, not goodness.

## Article 29 — Failure
Incomplete information shall not transfer authority.

- Missing information shall not imply assumptions.
- Failure to establish sufficiency shall not permit invention.
- Qualified outcomes are preferable to fabricated certainty.
- Absence of information shall not justify the creation of information.

## Article 30 — Effects and Dependency
Effects may create dependencies, but dependencies do not create authority.

- Dependencies shall not create authority.
- Consumption does not imply ownership.
- Participation does not imply jurisdiction.
- Authority belongs to ownership rather than consequence.
